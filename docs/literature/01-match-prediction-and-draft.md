# Literature Review: Match Outcome Prediction and Draft/Pick Recommendation in MOBA Games (2016–2026)

> 调研日期：2026-07-14。方法：网络检索并核实（venue 页面、arXiv、IEEE/ACM/AAAI 数字图书馆）。
> 覆盖游戏：Dota 2、League of Legends (LoL)、Honor of Kings (HoK)。
> 注意：不同论文的数据集（公开局 vs 职业赛、分段、补丁）差异极大，准确率数字不可跨论文直接比较。

## 1. Pre-match win prediction from draft composition (picks/bans)

### Key papers

| Paper | Venue/Year | Contribution | Reported metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Semenov et al., "Performance of Machine Learning Algorithms in Predicting Game Outcome from Drafts in Dota 2" | AIST 2016 (Springer LNCS) | First systematic comparison of Naive Bayes, logistic regression, GBDT, and Factorization Machines on draft-only prediction; FM wins because it captures pairwise hero interactions in a learned latent space. | AUC 0.706 (normal skill) degrading to 0.670 (high) and 0.660 (very high skill) — an early, explicit demonstration that draft signal shrinks as player skill rises. |
| L. Hanke, L. Chaimowicz, "A Recommender System for Hero Line-Ups in MOBA Games" | AIIDE 2017 | Mined hero-synergy association rules (Apriori) from Dota 2 matches; a winner-prediction NN used as evaluation backbone. | Claims 88.63% winner-prediction accuracy on public match data — an outlier figure widely regarded as reflecting skewed public-data setups rather than true draft signal (contrast with Semenov/DraftRec). |
| D.-H. Kim, C. Lee, K.-S. Chung, "A Confidence-Calibrated MOBA Game Winner Predictor" | IEEE CoG 2020 ([arXiv:2006.15521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15521)) | Argues MOBA winner prediction has high input-dependent (aleatoric) noise; proposes a data-uncertainty-aware calibration method for LoL draft-based predictors. | Expected Calibration Error 0.57% vs 1.11% for temperature scaling; frames the accuracy ceiling as irreducible label noise. |
| H. Lee et al., "DraftRec: Personalized Draft Recommendation for Winning in MOBA Games" | WWW 2022 ([arXiv:2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750), [code](https://github.com/dojeon-ai/DraftRec)) | Hierarchical two-level Transformer (player network over per-player match history + match network over all 10 players) jointly trained for champion recommendation and match-outcome prediction; also the reference modern dataset (~280K top-0.1% LoL matches, Diamond–Challenger). | Match-outcome accuracy **54.6–55.2%** (per official repo) — the clearest published evidence of the ~55% draft-only ceiling on high-skill, matchmaking-balanced data; HR@10 ≈ 88–90% for pick recommendation. |
| A. J. Bisberg, E. Ferrara, "GCN-WP — Semi-Supervised Graph Convolutional Networks for Win Prediction in Esports" | IEEE CoG 2022 ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9893671/)) | Models a whole professional LoL league season as a graph (matches/teams as neighborhoods, 30+ pre-match features) and uses semi-supervised GCN to predict across leagues — first cross-league transfer setup. | Claims SOTA vs. skill-rating (Elo/TrueSkill-style) and ML baselines for pro LoL. |
| K. Akhmedov, A. H. Phan, "Machine learning models for DOTA 2 outcomes prediction" | [arXiv:2106.01782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01782), 2021 | Systematic pre-match feature study (drafts + player stats) with classic ML pipelines on Dota 2. | Pre-match predictors cluster around ~55–60% accuracy when data is not skewed. |

Related earlier baseline work often cited: A. Summerville, M. Cook, B. Steenhuisen, "Draft-Analysis of the Ancients: Predicting Draft Picks in DotA 2" ([AIIDE 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12899)) — predicts *what humans will pick next* in pro drafts (sequence modeling of the draft itself, useful for ban/counter-pick anticipation rather than outcome prediction).

### The accuracy ceiling and why

- Converging evidence puts **draft-only pre-match accuracy at ~52–58%** on balanced, high-skill data: DraftRec ~55%; industry tools ([LoLDraftAI](https://loldraftai.com/) ~55–57%, one model reports 56.7% draft-only and 57.7% with runes; [TechLabs pro-LoL draft study](https://techlabs-aachen.medium.com/determining-win-percentage-from-draft-phase-in-a-professional-league-of-legends-game-59ea4e4d5c55) similar range); Semenov's FM AUC ≈ 0.66 at very-high skill.
- Reasons the literature itself gives: (1) **matchmaking actively balances teams**, removing skill signal; (2) execution, communication, and in-game decisions dominate outcome variance and are invisible at draft time (Kim et al. 2020 formalize this as input-dependent label noise); (3) **patch non-stationarity** — hero/champion balance changes invalidate learned synergy weights every few weeks; (4) skill-tier dependence — signal is larger in low-skill public data.
- Reported figures far above this range (70–89%) come from **skewed setups**: public-match datasets with strong rating imbalance, leakage of player/team identity, or pro data where team strength (not the draft) carries the signal — e.g., the industry [STRATZ TI8 predictor](https://medium.com/stratz/using-ai-to-predict-dota-2-the-international-8-games-at-77-accuracy-5485a6399f2f) (77.4% on The International 2018) uses team/player identity and historical form, not draft alone.

## 2. In-match / real-time win probability prediction

| Paper | Venue/Year | Contribution | Reported metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| V. J. Hodge, S. Devlin, N. Sephton, F. Block, P. Cowling, A. Drachen, "Win Prediction in Multi-Player Esports: Live Professional Match Prediction" | IEEE Transactions on Games 13(4), 2021 ([PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/152931/1/Win_Prediction_in_Multi_Player_Esports_Live_Professional_Match_Prediction.pdf)) | First professional-level *live* Dota 2 prediction model, trialed in production at a major esports tournament (with ESL); careful feature engineering over telemetry, trained on pro/semi-pro matches to avoid skill-transfer bias. | Up to **85% accuracy after 5 minutes** of gameplay. |
| A. L. C. Silva, G. L. Pappa, L. Chaimowicz, "Continuous Outcome Prediction of LoL Competitive Matches Using Recurrent Neural Networks" | SBGames 2018 ([PDF](https://www.sbgames.org/sbgames2018/files/papers/ComputacaoShort/188226.pdf)) | Minute-by-minute RNN over gold/XP/objective time series in competitive LoL; shows win probability curves can reveal composition power spikes. | 63.9% accuracy at 5 min rising to 83.5% at 25 min. |
| L. Yu, D. Zhang et al., "MOBA-Slice: A Time Slice Based Evaluation Framework of Relative Advantage between Teams in MOBA Games" | CGW@IJCAI 2018 ([arXiv:1807.08360](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08360)) | AlphaGo-value-network-style discounted evaluation function over Dota 2 time slices; predicts both outcome and remaining game time. | Beats Valve's commercial Dota Plus Assistant by 3.7% at result prediction. |
| Z. Yang et al., "Interpretable Real-Time Win Prediction for Honor of Kings" | [arXiv:2008.06313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06313) (2020; later IEEE ToG) | Two-Stage Spatial-Temporal Network (TSSTN) separating spatial (lane/feature) and temporal stages so predictions decompose into per-feature contributions; deployed for esports live-streaming commentary. | Effective accuracy + interpretability; feature attributions validated in real broadcasts. |
| C. Zhao et al., "Winning Tracker: A New Model for Real-time Winning Prediction in MOBA Games" | WWW 2022 ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3485447.3512274), NetEase Fuxi) | Unified framework with offense/defense confrontation extractors, trajectory (movement) representation, hierarchical attention for team strategy, and multi-task short-term/long-term objectives; deployed in a commercial MOBA. | Outperforms SOTA baselines on a large real-world dataset (industrially deployed). |
| A. Katona et al., "Time to Die: Death Prediction in Dota 2 using Deep Learning" | IEEE CoG 2019 ([arXiv:1906.03939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03939)); follow-up "Time to Die 2" in Machine Learning with Applications 2023 | Micro-scale real-time prediction: which of the 10 players dies within 5 seconds, from pro-match telemetry — supports broadcast direction and moment-to-moment win-probability shifts. | Precision 0.377 / recall 0.725 on ~1%-positive events. |
| J. B. S. Junior, C. E. C. Campelo, "League of Legends: Real-Time Result Prediction" | [arXiv:2309.02449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02449) (2023) | Stage-wise comparison of classic ML/GBM models over live LoL variables. | LightGBM best: 81.6% average accuracy at 60–80% elapsed game time; LR/GB better in early game. |

Also relevant: A. Bisberg et al., "Analyzing the Differences between Professional and Amateur Esports through Win Probability" ([WWW 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3485447.3512277)) — uses win-probability models as an analytical instrument to contrast pro vs. amateur play. A 2025 example of the "high accuracy = late-game features" pattern: Hojaji et al., "Deep learning techniques for identifying KPIs in League of Legends" (Computers in Human Behavior Reports, 2025; 154K matches, 97% accuracy using full-match KPIs — accuracy here reflects end-state features like turrets lost, not early prediction).

**Consensus shape of the curve:** ~55% at 0:00 (draft only) → ~70–75% by 10 min → 85%+ late game; the informative research questions have therefore shifted to interpretability (TSSTN), granular event prediction (Time to Die), movement/spatial signal (Winning Tracker), and calibration rather than raw accuracy.

## 3. Draft recommendation / ban-pick strategy

| Paper | Venue/Year | Contribution | Reported metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z. Chen et al., "The Art of Drafting: A Team-Oriented Hero Recommendation System for MOBA Games" (**DraftArtist**) | ACM RecSys 2018 ([arXiv:1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)) | Formulates drafting as a two-player combinatorial game; MCTS with win-rate value estimation recommends the next hero to maximize final team win probability, explicitly accounting for opponents' future picks. | Simulation-based win-rate gains over greedy/association-rule baselines (Dota 2 data). |
| S. Chen, M. Zhu, D. Ye, W. Zhang, Q. Fu, W. Yang, "Which Heroes to Pick? Learning to Draft in MOBA Games with Neural Networks and Tree Search" (**JueWuDraft**) | IEEE Transactions on Games 13(4), 2021 ([arXiv:2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171), Tencent) | AlphaZero-style drafting: policy+value network trained with MCTS self-play; long-term value mechanism for best-of-N series where heroes can't be reused across games (HoK). | Win rates vs. baselines: 0.981 vs random, 0.677 vs highest-win-rate heuristic, **0.580 vs DraftArtist**, 0.516 vs its own no-search variant. |
| H. Lee et al., **DraftRec** | WWW 2022 (see §1) | Only major work to make draft recommendation *personalized* — conditions on each player's champion history/proficiency, not just abstract team synergy; hierarchical Transformer serves recommendation and outcome prediction jointly. | HR@10 ≈ 88–90%, outcome accuracy ~55% (LoL, top-tier). |
| L. Hanke, L. Chaimowicz | AIIDE 2017 (see §1) | Association-rule (Apriori) synergy mining as an interpretable, lightweight recommender. | Up to 74.9% "recommendation success rate" under their evaluation protocol. |
| D. Ye et al., "Towards Playing Full MOBA Games with Deep Reinforcement Learning" (**JueWu**) | NeurIPS 2020 ([paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/file/06d5ae105ea1bea4d800bc96491876e9-Paper.pdf)) | Full-game HoK AI over a 40-hero pool; drafting handled by MCTS + value network as a subcomponent of the agent pipeline — drafting shown to matter for superhuman play at scale. | 95.2% win rate vs. professionals (40/42 games). |
| "Champion Recommendation in League of Legends Using Machine Learning" | ICCS 2023 ([PDF](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2023/papers/140760152.pdf)) | Recent classic-ML champion recommender using synergy/counter features per role. | Outcome classification accuracy >0.70 reported (public-data regime). |

Industry reference: **OpenAI Five** (Berner et al., [arXiv:1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680), 2019) selected drafts for its restricted 17-hero pool by evaluating its own win-probability estimate over all ~4.9M possible lineups — an early large-scale demonstration of value-network-driven drafting.

**Method arc:** association rules (2017) → MCTS over a learned value function (2018) → neural policy + tree search with self-play and multi-game series values (2021) → Transformer sequence models with personalization (2022). Post-2022 academic output shifts toward personalization, deployment, and LLM-adjacent assistants rather than new search algorithms.

## 4. Team composition synergy / counter modeling

| Paper | Venue/Year | Contribution | Reported metrics |
|---|---|---|---|
| J. Kim, B. Keegan, S. Park, A. Oh, "The Proficiency-Congruency Dilemma: Virtual Team Design and Performance in Multiplayer Online Games" | CHI 2016 ([arXiv:1512.08321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08321)) | Large-scale (3.36M LoL players) empirical study of the trade-off between picking what you're good at vs. what the team needs. | Both proficiency and congruency raise win probability; **individual proficiency dominates team-composition fit** — a key argument for personalization in recommenders. |
| A. Semenov et al. (FM) | AIST 2016 (see §1) | Factorization Machines as implicit pairwise synergy/counter matrices — the canonical "interaction matrix factorization" baseline. | AUC 0.66–0.71 by skill tier. |
| L. Gong et al., "OptMatch: Optimized Matchmaking via Modeling the High-Order Interactions on the Arena" | KDD 2020 ADS ([PDF](https://linxiagong.github.io/OptMatch/KDD2020_OptMatch.pdf), [code](https://github.com/fuxiAIlab/OptMatch), NetEase) | Learns hero embeddings in separate **synergy and suppression spaces** plus gravity-like high-order team interactions; two-stage offline-learning/online-planning matchmaking framework. | Evaluated on 4 real-world datasets incl. 623K LoL matches (145 heroes); embeddings shown to cluster heroes by role; online experiment deployed. |
| Y. Gu et al., "NeuralAC: Learning Cooperation and Competition Effects for Match Outcome Prediction" | AAAI 2021 ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16528)) | Attention over all intra-team (cooperation) and inter-team (competition) pairwise latent interactions — learns *which* synergy/counter pairs matter and provides interpretability (recovers known Dota 2 counter relationships). | Outperforms FM/deep baselines on game datasets incl. Dota 2. |
| Y. Gu et al., "MassNE: Exploring Higher-Order Interactions with Marginal Effect for Massive Battle Outcome Prediction" | WWW 2023 ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3543507.3583390), [code](https://github.com/firepd/MassNE)) | Extends synergy/suppression modeling to massive unit compositions with monotonic marginal-utility modules (unit value depends on counts). | SOTA on StarCraft II-generated battle datasets; framework applicable to MOBA lineups. |
| Z. Zhang et al., "CEMOP: Enhancing MOBA match outcome predictions by understanding teammates and opponents effects" | ISCAIS 2024 (SPIE proc. 13210) | Match-context encoder injecting teammate/opponent influence into individual player representations before team aggregation. | Beats SOTA on three real-world MOBA datasets. |

(GCN-WP in §1 is the main published GNN-on-esports-structure work; most "GNN for champion synergy" activity remains in preprints and open-source projects rather than top venues — a genuine gap.)

## 5. Open gaps and limitations acknowledged in the literature

1. **The draft-only ceiling is real but under-theorized.** ~55% on balanced high-skill data (DraftRec) vs. 70%+ on skewed public data (Hanke & Chaimowicz-style setups); Kim et al. (CoG 2020) is nearly alone in treating it formally as aleatoric noise. There is no standard benchmark separating draft signal from player-skill leakage.
2. **Patch non-stationarity.** Nearly every paper notes hero balance patches invalidate learned interaction weights; almost none propose continual/meta-learning solutions or evaluate across patches. This is arguably the biggest deployment obstacle.
3. **Circular evaluation of recommenders.** DraftArtist, JueWuDraft, and DraftRec all evaluate recommendations with a *learned* win-rate predictor (or self-play against it); true causal effect of following recommendations (A/B tests with humans) is untested in the academic literature.
4. **No shared benchmarks or datasets.** Results span Dota 2 public matches, LoL ranked tiers, pro leagues, and proprietary HoK data; cross-paper accuracy comparisons are essentially meaningless. DraftRec's released LoL dataset is the closest thing to a standard.
5. **Personalization is underexplored.** CHI 2016 showed proficiency beats congruency, yet only DraftRec conditions recommendations on player history; team-level synergy models (OptMatch, NeuralAC) ignore who is piloting the hero.
6. **Calibration and uncertainty are rarely reported** despite betting/broadcast use cases — only Kim et al. 2020 addresses it directly; most papers report only accuracy/AUC.
7. **Ban modeling is thin.** Most systems recommend picks; explicit modeling of bans and opponent-intent anticipation (beyond MCTS opponent simulation and Summerville's pick prediction) is scarce.
8. **Pro-scene data scarcity.** Hodge et al. emphasize models trained on public matches transfer poorly to professional play, but pro datasets are small (hundreds–thousands of games per patch), limiting deep models.
9. **Cross-league/cross-game generalization** raised by GCN-WP remains mostly open, as does transfer between MOBA titles.
10. **Post-2022 top-venue drought for drafting.** After DraftRec/Winning Tracker (WWW 2022) and MassNE (WWW 2023), recent work drifts to smaller venues (ISCAIS 2024, SPIE) and LLM-assistant applications (e.g., LoL match-data summarization for LLMs, Applied Sciences 2025) — suggesting room for a strong modern contribution (e.g., patch-robust, calibrated, personalized draft models).
